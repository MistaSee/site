01 / Tabletop systems · In development

Stormscar: The Ashtide Rises

Designing a world that doesn't wait for the players

A post-apocalyptic fantasy campaign built around competing factions, persistent relationships and consequences that outlast individual encounters. Developed from a world-building project begun in 2021.

My design separates renown (how widely known players are) from reputation (how factions regard their actions). Asymmetric faction relationships and objective countdowns allow events to advance even when players choose not to intervene. A knock-on matrix connects consequences across factions, and information access distinguishes recorded knowledge from first-hand discovery.

Player decisions + independent faction objectives
↓

↓
Consequences and changed world state

Next research direction: explore digital characters with bounded generative dialogue, grounded in their knowledge, motivations and the world's mechanically enforced rules. This is a proposed digital experiment, not an implemented feature.

02 / Digital game · Playable vertical slice

Lantern Bay

Restore infrastructure
↓
Light creates safety — and attracts danger
↓
Investigate, experiment, understand

Learning through the mechanics, not the lecture

A maritime mystery combining exploration, resource management and atmospheric horror. The infrastructure that protects the town also creates a new threat, turning restoration into a strategic and narrative problem.

The signal puzzles draw on real physics, including harmonics and phase relationships. Players can make progress without fully understanding the science, but deeper understanding is designed to lead to better outcomes.

I direct the creative process, define mechanics and acceptance criteria, and delegate technical implementation to AI agents. The substantially complete vertical slice is undergoing quality assurance. It is not commercially released.

03 / Professional interaction design

Pick-a-Part

Raspberry Pi Foundation

Explore the interactive toolkit

Agency without a recipe

I designed the interface and interaction model for an open-ended physical computing toolkit. Young people can define their own Python-powered gadgets rather than follow a predetermined project.

The interface brings their choices and decisions together in one place so they can refer back to them as they build. The aim is to reduce the burden of remembering earlier decisions without taking creative control away from the learner.

The design builds on feedback from an earlier branching platformer pathway. Instead of choosing between predefined branches, learners have a toolkit for creating their own outcomes.

04 / Experimental game · Early design

Escape Condition

Uncertainty as a mechanic

An investigation game exploring fallible witness memories, weighted confidence and changing accounts. The design investigates how new evidence might affect what a witness believes, remembers or reports, rather than treating every conversation as a fixed dialogue tree.

Currently a design-stage exploration, not a playable or released game.

05 / Proposed research

Human judgement and AI-directed creativity

Community Fellow · SOAS University of London

I'm exploring what happens to creative authorship when AI handles much of a project's technical implementation. Using Lantern Bay as a case study, I have developed a proposed playtesting methodology that examines player experience before disclosing AI involvement, then asks how that knowledge affects their assessment.

Another strand explores the relationship between human creative decisions, implementation and revision histories. These are research proposals, not completed studies or established findings.
