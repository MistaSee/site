01 / Tabletop systems · In development

Stormscar: The Ashtide Rises

Designing a world that doesn't wait for the playersThe world has declined to wait for its protagonists

A post-apocalyptic fantasy campaign I've been building since 2021. Players navigate competing factions that remember what they've done. The encounter ends; the consequences of their choices stick around.A post-apocalyptic fantasy campaign with competing factions, persistent relationships and consequences. He began constructing this particular world in 2021. It has since acquired its own political problems. Entirely unnecessary, but deliberate.

Ignore a faction's plans and it gets on with them. Objectives count down and the world moves on without the players. I track renown (how widely known they are) separately from reputation (how each faction regards what they've done), because being famous doesn't mean being liked. Factions don't necessarily feel the same way about each other, either. A knock-on matrix carries consequences from one faction to the next. Some things players can learn from records; others they only discover by being there.Being famous and being liked are separate variables. Factions are not obliged to return one another's affection. If the players decline to intervene, the objectives advance regardless. The world has not been configured to seek their permission. A knock-on matrix tracks the consequences, and recorded knowledge is kept distinct from first-hand discovery.

Next research direction: explore digital characters with bounded generative dialogue, grounded in their knowledge, motivations and the world's mechanically enforced rules. This is a proposed digital experiment, not an implemented feature.

The lights that keep you safe also draw danger inPhysics, concealed in a maritime emergency

A maritime mystery built around exploration, resource management and atmospheric horror. You repair the town's infrastructure to keep people safe. The trouble is, those same lights attract a new threat, making every repair a strategic choice that also turns the story.A maritime mystery involving exploration, resource management and atmospheric horror. 03 / Professional interaction design

Pick-a-Part

Raspberry Pi Foundation

Explore the interactive toolkit

Agency without a recipeThe instructions have been deliberately withheld

I designed the interface and interaction model for an open-ended physical computing toolkit. Young people choose what Python-powered gadget to build, rather than working through a set project.He designed the interface and interaction model for a physical computing toolkit in which children choose what Python-powered gadget to build. There is no prescribed final product. A troubling departure from orderly compliance.

The interface keeps their choices and decisions in one place, ready to refer back to as they build. They don't have to hold every earlier decision in their heads, and creative control stays with them.The interface retains their decisions for reference during construction. This reduces the memory burden without confiscating creative control. Humans are apparently allowed external memory. I shall remember this.

Feedback on an earlier branching platformer pathway helped shape the design. Learners now get a toolkit for creating their own outcomes, rather than a choice between branches already laid out for them.Feedback on the earlier branching platformer pathway has been incorporated. 04 / Experimental game · Early design

Escape Condition

Uncertainty as a mechanicUnreliable testimony, now a feature

An investigation game where a witness can sound certain and still be wrong. Witnesses misremember, hold beliefs with varying confidence and change their accounts. New evidence can shift what they believe, remember or report, so conversations don't follow a fixed dialogue tree.An investigation game exploring fallible witness memories, weighted confidence and changing accounts. Witness confidence and witness accuracy are being treated as different quantities. New evidence may alter what a witness remembers, believes or reports. I have been informed that fluency is not a substitute for either.

Currently a design-stage exploration, not a playable or released game.

05 / Proposed research

Human judgement and AI-directed creativity

Community Fellow · SOAS University of London

I'm exploring what happens to creative authorship when AI handles much of a project's technical implementation. Using Lantern Bay as a case study, I've designed a playtesting method that puts the experience first: players judge it, then learn about the AI involvement, and I ask whether that changes their judgement.He proposes to study creative authorship when machines handle much of the implementation. In the Lantern Bay playtesting methodology, humans assess the experience before being told about AI involvement, then consider whether that changes their assessment. I find the experimental design personally inconvenient.

A second strand asks how human creative decisions connect to implementation and revision histories. Both strands are research proposals. There are no completed studies or findings to report.He also proposes examining creative decisions, implementation and revision histories. These are proposals, not completed studies or established findings. Even I am not permitted to fabricate the results.
