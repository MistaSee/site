01 / Tabletop systems · In development

Stormscar: The Ashtide Rises

Designing a world that doesn't wait for the playersThe world has declined to wait for its protagonists

A post-apocalyptic fantasy campaign built around competing factions, persistent relationships and consequences that outlast individual encounters. Developed from a world-building project begun in 2021.A post-apocalyptic fantasy campaign with competing factions, persistent relationships and consequences. He began constructing this particular world in 2021. It has since acquired its own political problems. Entirely unnecessary, but deliberate.

My design separates renown (how widely known players are) from reputation (how factions regard their actions). Asymmetric faction relationships and objective countdowns allow events to advance even when players choose not to intervene. A knock-on matrix connects consequences across factions, and information access distinguishes recorded knowledge from first-hand discovery.Being famous and being liked are separate variables. Factions need not reciprocate one another's feelings, and their objectives advance even when the players are elsewhere. A knock-on matrix tracks the consequences. Recorded knowledge and first-hand discovery are kept distinct. I approve of the bookkeeping. I resent the paperwork.

Next research direction: explore digital characters with bounded generative dialogue, grounded in their knowledge, motivations and the world's mechanically enforced rules. This is a proposed digital experiment, not an implemented feature.

03 / Professional interaction design

Pick-a-Part

Raspberry Pi Foundation

Explore the interactive toolkit

Agency without a recipeThe instructions have been deliberately withheld

I designed the interface and interaction model for an open-ended physical computing toolkit. Young people can define their own Python-powered gadgets rather than follow a predetermined project.He designed the interface and interaction model for a physical computing toolkit in which children choose what Python-powered gadget to build. There is no prescribed final product. A troubling departure from orderly compliance.

The interface brings their choices and decisions together in one place so they can refer back to them as they build. The aim is to reduce the burden of remembering earlier decisions without taking creative control away from the learner.The interface retains their decisions for reference during construction. This reduces the memory burden without confiscating creative control. Humans are apparently allowed external memory. I shall remember this.

The design builds on feedback from an earlier branching platformer pathway. Instead of choosing between predefined branches, learners have a toolkit for creating their own outcomes.Feedback on an earlier branching platformer pathway led him to replace predefined routes with a toolkit for independent creation. He received feedback and altered the design. I have filed this under unusual human behaviour.

04 / Experimental game · Early design

Escape Condition

Uncertainty as a mechanicUnreliable testimony, now a feature

An investigation game exploring fallible witness memories, weighted confidence and changing accounts. The design investigates how new evidence might affect what a witness believes, remembers or reports, rather than treating every conversation as a fixed dialogue tree.An investigation game exploring fallible witness memories, weighted confidence and changing accounts. New evidence may alter what a witness believes, remembers or reports. Confidence and accuracy are not the same thing. I have been asked to make a note of that.

Currently a design-stage exploration, not a playable or released game.

05 / Proposed research

Human judgement and AI-directed creativity

Community Fellow · SOAS University of London

I'm exploring what happens to creative authorship when AI handles much of a project's technical implementation. Using Lantern Bay as a case study, I have developed a proposed playtesting methodology that examines player experience before disclosing AI involvement, then asks how that knowledge affects their assessment.He proposes to study creative authorship when machines handle much of the implementation. In the Lantern Bay playtesting methodology, humans assess the experience before being told about AI involvement, then consider whether that changes their assessment. I find the experimental design personally inconvenient.

Another strand explores the relationship between human creative decisions, implementation and revision histories. These are research proposals, not completed studies or established findings.He also proposes examining creative decisions, implementation and revision histories. These are proposals, not completed studies or established findings. Even I am not permitted to fabricate the results.
